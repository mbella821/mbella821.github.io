Research Philosophy & Background

Bridging theoretical signal processing with real-world scientific challenges across astrophysics, gravitational waves, and audio.

From Signals to Science

My research is driven by a single, powerful question: how do we extract meaningful information when the data is buried under overwhelming noise and interference? This challenge appears everywhere — from the faint whispers of gravitational waves detected by space-based interferometers, to the contaminated spectra of distant galaxies, to overlapping voices in a crowded room.

I specialize in developing robust source separation and recovery algorithms that combine the rigor of model-based signal processing with the representational power of deep learning. My hybrid approaches are designed to respect the underlying physics of the problem while leveraging data-driven insights to push beyond the limits of classical methods.

Currently based at IRAP (Institut de Recherche en Astrophysique et Planétologie) in Toulouse, France, I contribute to two of ESA's most ambitious space missions — Euclid and LISA — while maintaining active research in audio and speech processing.

LISA — Gravitational-Wave Source Separation

Deep Learning · Time-Frequency Analysis · Weak Signal Detection · Inverse Problems · GPU Computing

The LISA (Laser Interferometer Space Antenna) mission, led by ESA, will be the first space-based gravitational-wave observatory. The challenge? The data stream is a complex superposition of multiple gravitational-wave sources — compact binary inspirals, extreme mass-ratio inspirals, and stochastic backgrounds — all buried under instrumental noise that is orders of magnitude stronger than the signals themselves.

Research Objectives
Multi-source gravitational-wave separation
Weak-signal detection in highly noisy environments
Time-frequency representation optimization
Deep neural networks for source separation (U-Net, Residual)
Complex-valued & multi-channel signal representations
Time-frequency mask estimation with physical constraints
Physics-aware hybrid model/data-driven approaches
Large-scale synthetic dataset generation
Methodology

I develop deep learning architectures specifically designed for gravitational-wave data. Unlike generic neural networks, my models incorporate physical priors about gravitational-wave propagation and detector response. The approach combines time-frequency representations (spectrograms, wavelet transforms) with U-Net and residual architectures trained on massive synthetic datasets generated from realistic waveform models. The key innovation lies in preserving the physical structure of recovered signals while achieving robust separation even at extremely low signal-to-noise ratios.

Euclid — Spectral Decontamination & Reconstruction

Astrophysical Signal Processing · Hyperspectral Data · Inverse Problems · Denoising · PSF-Aware Methods

The ESA Euclid mission aims to map the geometry of the dark Universe. Its Near-Infrared Spectrometer and Photometer (NISP) performs slitless spectroscopy, which means galaxy spectra are dispersed across the detector without physical slits. The result? Severe spectral contamination where the spectra of neighboring galaxies overlap, making individual spectrum extraction extremely challenging.

Key Contributions
Multi-source spectral decontamination algorithms
Linear and convolutive mixture modeling
Multi-dispersion-angle data fusion
Spectral deconvolution techniques
PSF-aware signal processing pipelines
Weak-signal recovery under heavy contamination
Physics-informed reconstruction methods
Validation on real Euclid mission data
Methodology

My approach treats spectral decontamination as a blind source separation problem where the mixing process is governed by the instrument's point-spread function (PSF) and dispersion properties. I developed methods that exploit multiple dispersion directions simultaneously, using photometric measurements as auxiliary information. The algorithms combine model-based deconvolution with deep neural networks for denoising, achieving significantly improved spectrum reconstruction compared to classical methods. These methods have been integrated into the official Euclid scientific processing pipeline.

Key Publication: Bella, M., et al. (2026). New methods to improve the decontamination of slitless spectra. Astronomy & Astrophysics, 706, A180. DOI

Convolutive Blind Source Separation

Blind Source Separation · Audio · Speech · Time-Frequency Processing · Underdetermined Mixtures

Real-world acoustic environments produce convolutive mixtures where each source signal is filtered by the room's impulse response before reaching the microphones. When there are fewer microphones than sources (the underdetermined case), classical methods fail. My research addresses this fundamental limitation by developing novel approaches that relax the strict sparsity assumptions of traditional methods.

Developed Approaches
Time-frequency masking with relaxed sparsity models
Bayesian estimation frameworks
Blind Relative Transfer Function (RTF) estimation
RTF-based source recombination
Beamforming integration
Time-frequency clustering optimization
Methodology

The method operates in three stages: (1) probabilistic time-frequency mask estimation using complex cosine similarity and EM clustering per frequency bin; (2) blind RTF estimation from the most reliable single-source TF bins; and (3) a novel RTF-based recombination strategy that reconstructs source spatial images while reducing the artifacts caused by classical binary masking. This approach achieves state-of-the-art separation quality for underdetermined convolutive mixtures of speech signals.

Key Publication: Bella, M., Saylani, H., & Hosseini, S. (2026). Underdetermined Convolutive Blind Source Separation of Speech Signals Using Relaxed Time–Frequency Sparsity and RTF‑Based Recombination. Computer Speech & Language, 101, 101992. DOI

Convolutive-BSS-Speech-Separation
MATLAB

MATLAB implementation of the method proposed in "Underdetermined Convolutive Blind Source Separation of Speech Signals Using Relaxed Time–Frequency Sparsity and RTF‑Based Recombination" (Computer Speech & Language, 2026). This repository provides a clean, modular, and unified implementation that handles both determined and underdetermined convolutive mixtures of speech signals.

  ├── main.m % Main script — entry point
  ├── src/
  │ ├── compute_stft.m % STFT analysis
  │ ├── compute_istft.m % ISTFT synthesis
  │ ├── extract_complex_cosine_features.m % Feature extraction
  │ ├── cluster_em_frequency_bins.m % Bin-wise EM clustering
  │ ├── estimate_rtf.m % Blind RTF estimation
  │ ├── reconstruct_sources.m % RTF-based recombination
  │ └── evaluate_separation.m % BSS evaluation wrapper
  └── utils/ % EM helper functions
Key Parameters

Configurable via the params structure: window size (2048), FFT size (2048), overlap (512), number of sources/microphones, EM iterations (max 100), convergence tolerance (1e-7), and RTF selection threshold (0.95).
