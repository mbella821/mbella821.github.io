01 / Perspective

One research question.
Different kinds of signals.

How can we recover individual sources when their contributions overlap and the observations are incomplete or noisy?

My work connects mathematical modelling, statistical signal processing and machine learning. I build on what is known about a measurement — its geometry, instrument response or spatial structure — and develop algorithms that can be tested against reference signals and real observations.

My background spans Euclid spectral processing at IRAP, speech separation in reverberant environments, and simulation-based research for LISA. Alongside research, I teach scientific programming and signal and image processing.

01 / Slitless spectroscopy
Euclid / NISPPublished methods

Untangling
galaxy spectra.

Recovering individual spectra from slitless observations where neighbouring astronomical objects contaminate one another.

Multi-direction mixing models and deconvolution, with contributions to Euclid’s SIR spectroscopic pipeline.

Inverse problemsPSF modellingPython / MATLAB
Approach & contribution

I develop spatial- and Fourier-domain separation methods that combine multiple dispersion directions with photometric information and the instrument response. The work includes algorithm development, simulated-data evaluation and application to Euclid observations. The published A&A study evaluates four methods on realistic simulated data.

02 / Gravitational-wave signals
LISAOngoing research

Recovering faint
gravitational signals.

Separating massive black hole binary and galactic binary waveforms from simulated multichannel observations.

A research workflow combining waveform generation, time-frequency masks and neural-network reconstruction.

U-NetComplex masksTensorFlow / Keras
Approach & evaluation

I explore time-domain and spectrogram networks, wavelet methods and multichannel filtering using simulated A/E/T data. Evaluation compares reconstructed waveforms with known targets through overlap and reconstruction SNR, with oracle masks as reference benchmarks. This is simulation-based method development, using an approximate response model.

03 / Multichannel speech
Audio & speechPaper + code

Separating voices.
Preserving structure.

Blind source separation for reverberant mixtures, including cases with fewer microphones than speakers.

Relaxed sparsity assumptions, probabilistic masking and relative-transfer-function recombination.

Bayesian estimationBeamformingMATLAB
Approach & contribution

Complex cosine features support frequency-wise clustering and mask estimation. Blindly estimated relative transfer functions then guide reconstruction of each source’s spatial image. The methods address artifacts introduced by time-frequency masking and are evaluated with source-separation criteria.
